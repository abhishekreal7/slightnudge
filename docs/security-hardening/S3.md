# S3 — atomic publication

The asserted non-atomic fallback is **not present at baseline**. Forced EPERM on a temp-copy rename already refuses, leaves the original byte-identical and removes the publication temp. `S3-before-complete.log/json` records that passing baseline property; no fallback was invented or added to create a failure.

Two new failures were proved before this fix:

| Product hunk | Before / after proof |
|---|---|
| Replace one-shot UTF-8 writeSync with a complete Buffer write loop, refusing zero progress | Injected short writes published only `rep` before. After, all bytes including the multibyte checkmark are published; 12 short writes complete the payload. |
| Reduce Windows-lock retry schedule to 25/50/100ms | Six rename attempts before; one initial attempt plus three retries after. Permanent failure still refuses and preserves original bytes. |

After: `S3-after.log/json`, exit 0. The existing unique temp, flush/fsync, close, atomic rename and refuse-without-overwrite behavior is retained. Non-lock rename errors already refuse without retries; known transient lock errors receive at most three retries. No non-atomic overwrite fallback exists. All faults are injected only in the designated scratch copy; real projects are never patched or renamed by the experiment.

This write-path change is its own commit, `core: complete atomic writes and bound rename retries`. Every product hunk is in S3-product-hunks.patch. Power-loss durability of the parent directory, filesystem hardware behavior and a same-user directory-swap attacker are not tested here.
